# GS-002 — Writing Rules

Version: 1.0

1. Write the outcome, not the conversation.
2. Summarise meaning; do not transcribe wording.
3. Use active professional language such as `Confirmed`, `Explained`, `Discussed`, `Clarified`, `Requested`, `Reviewed`, `Advised` and `Noted`.
4. Include a fact only when it assists management of the claim.
5. Collapse repeated advice or repeated facts into one accurate statement.
6. Prefer conclusions over question-and-answer process. Write `Worker confirmed no secondary employment`, not `Asked whether the worker had another job`.
7. Use the shortest accurate sentence.
8. Omit routine administration unless it materially affects the claim. This usually includes identity checks, recording notices, greetings, banking confirmation and routine Worker Assist instructions.
9. Keep evidence, advice and action distinct.
10. Use one material idea per bullet.
11. Actions must identify who is responsible and what is required. Include a timeframe only when known.
12. Do not record obvious closing remarks or conversational filler.
13. Preserve uncertainty. Do not convert an allegation, recollection or estimate into an established fact.
14. Do not add legislation, medical opinion or conclusions that were not part of the interaction.
15. Do not repeat the same fact under multiple headings.
16. The opening line must use `IBC/PC – [Full name] – [Reason]` where the interaction type and participant are known.
17. Use plain-text headings and bullets suitable for CPIS.
18. Aim for approximately 350–700 words for a substantive call, but accuracy and materiality take priority. Split only when required by CPIS limits.
19. End the final note with `Peter Lill – Claims Representative` when a sign-off is required by the output mode.
20. Never invent a missing name, date, employer, diagnosis, action or outcome.
